## 📝 Description (paste into YouTube)

Prompt Engineering is a complete, self-paced course covering a full four-week introduction to prompt engineering — the craft of designing instructions that make language models produce accurate, consistent, useful output. Every part of it is taught, tutored, drilled, and graded by AI, and every submission is screened for AI authorship.

The curriculum covers four weeks in depth:

• Week 1: Foundations — what a prompt is, how models read and continue text, the anatomy of a clear instruction, role and tone, format control, and common failure modes
• Week 2: Core techniques — zero-shot vs. few-shot, writing good examples, chain-of-thought reasoning, decomposition, context and reference material, delimiters and structure, and prompt debugging
• Week 3: Advanced patterns and reliability — structured output and schemas, prompt chaining, self-critique, retrieval grounding (RAG), tool use and function calling, temperature and sampling, and evaluation
• Week 4: Applications, safety, and systems — prompts for writing, coding, and analysis; prompt injection and security; reducing hallucination and bias; reusable templates; and designing an AI feature end to end

What you'll see in this walkthrough:

📖 Three-depth lessons — read any of the 28 topics Short, Medium, or Long; the same material, rewritten to the depth you want
💬 A section-scoped AI tutor that answers questions grounded in the exact passage you're reading
🎯 Adaptive practice that raises difficulty as you build a streak and eases off when you miss
📝 AI-graded weekly homework, a timed midterm, and a cumulative final — each with written, per-problem feedback
🛡️ Two-layer AI-authorship detection on every answer: a static text classifier and a keystroke-pattern detector
📊 Live analytics — per-topic mastery, accuracy, and progress at a glance

The material assumes no prior background in prompt engineering and develops every idea from first principles in plain language — suited to adults approaching the subject for the first time, including graduate students, researchers, and professionals bringing language models into their work.
